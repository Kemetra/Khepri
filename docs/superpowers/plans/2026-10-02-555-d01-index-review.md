# #555 D-01: evidence-first index review

The owner's 2026-09-24 ruling on #555: no speculative indexes. An index lands only if PostgreSQL
`EXPLAIN ANALYZE` on realistic data shows a material benefit to an actual production query, and
each index that lands gets its own migration. This plan fixes the scale, the method and the
materiality threshold **before any plan is collected**, so none of them can be chosen after seeing
a number. The evidence goes in `2026-10-02-555-d01-index-evidence.md`.

## Candidates and the production statements they serve

Statements are captured as SQLAlchemy emits them (PostgreSQL dialect, via the real store methods
or the named module-level statement), never hand-written.

1. **`rca_memberships (account_id)`**. Served by
   - `PostgresMembershipStore.memberships_for_account` (`WHERE account_id = $1`);
   - `organizations_for_account`, a join of `rca_organizations` to `rca_memberships` on
     `account_id`, called on every shell page that renders the organization switcher
     (`shell_api`, `shell_comparison`, `shell_decisions`, `shell_invitations`);
   - the sub-select inside `owner_memberships_for_update(account_id)`, which runs
     `FOR UPDATE` during account disablement (`owner_reduction_outcome`).
   The `FOR UPDATE` benefit, if any, is a shorter statement inside the lock-holding transaction.
   PostgreSQL locks only the rows returned, so the lock footprint does not change, and this plan
   does not claim it does.
2. **`rra_fact_packages (owner_id, package_digest)`**. Served by `get_owned_package`
   (`WHERE package_digest = $1 AND owner_id = $2`), called from `semantic_view_adapter` whenever a
   historical run is read back.
3. **`rra_deletion_evidence (attempted_at)`**. Served by `purge_evidence_before`
   (`DELETE ... WHERE attempted_at < $1`), run by `DeletionEvidenceSweeper` in the retention sweep.
   Background work, lowest priority.

## Scale, and where each number comes from

**No governed count exists for organizations, accounts, memberships or packages.**
`KHEPRI-DEC-028` §Sizing and `KHEPRI-DEC-030` §"Not final capacity" defer all capacity to `OPS1-09`.
`KHEPRI-DEC-003` and `KHEPRI-DEC-029` leave the client count and observation period to a later
beta-authorization artifact. `KHEPRI-DEC-029`'s workload is 40 synthetic datasets submitted
sequentially and is explicitly "not a load, capacity, soak, or concurrency test".
`KHEPRI-DEC-007` and its `KHEPRI-BMK-001-sizing.yaml` are retired and are not cited as authority.

What governance does fix are the **retention rules that make rows accumulate**
(`KHEPRI-DEC-033`):

- fact packages live "with the analysis run that produced it, while the organization exists",
  with no inactivity expiry (§1 table);
- deletion evidence lives twelve months from the deletion event (§2, `OD-2`), after which the
  sweep purges it;
- the production target is Managed PostgreSQL **17** (`KHEPRI-DEC-028`).

Everything else below is therefore **a stated assumption, not a governed number**. They are chosen
on the generous side so that a "no benefit" verdict at hosted scale is conservative: a larger table
favours the index.

| Quantity | Beta scale | Hosted scale | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organizations | 25 | 5,000 | assumption (no governed count) |
| Accounts | 100 | 20,000 | assumption: about 4 accounts per organization |
| Memberships | 150 | 30,000 | assumption: about 1.5 organizations per account; 1 owner and the rest members per organization |
| Packages per owner | 50 | 100 | assumption: about 2 per week kept for a year or two, no expiry per `KHEPRI-DEC-033` §1 |
| Owners with packages | 20 | 2,000 | assumption |
| `rra_fact_packages` rows | 1,000 | 200,000 | product of the two rows above |
| Deletion evidence per day | 30 | 1,500 | assumption: 10 (beta) or 500 (hosted) deletion jobs a day at 3 evidence rows each (one input, report artifacts, retry attempts) |
| `rra_deletion_evidence` rows | about 11,000 | about 550,000 | evidence per day over the 365-day horizon (`KHEPRI-DEC-033` §2) |
| Sweep | daily, deletes the rows of one day | same | assumption: cadence is not governed; a more frequent sweep deletes fewer rows per pass but still pays the same scan |

The decision is made **at hosted scale**. Beta scale is reported for context and cannot by itself
land an index. A further 10x sensitivity run is reported separately and cannot land an index either.
It only shows how the verdict would move.

Row width: `rra_fact_packages.document` is `json` and out of line once large. Seeded documents are
about 8 KB of poorly compressible text so that the heap and TOAST shape is realistic, rather than
tiny rows that would make a sequential scan look artificially cheap.

Seeding takes the foreign-key parents out of play with `session_replication_role = replica` for the
three tables whose queries never touch the parents (`rra_fact_packages`, `rca_memberships`,
`rra_deletion_evidence`). `rca_organizations` is seeded for real because
`organizations_for_account` joins it.

## Environments

- Production is PostgreSQL 17 (`KHEPRI-DEC-028`); CI runs 17; this machine has **18.4** only.
- PG 18 adds B-tree skip scan, so on 18 a lookup by `account_id` alone can use the
  `(organization_id, account_id)` primary key. PG 17 cannot; its baseline is a sequential scan
  (or a full index scan, equally O(n)).
- Accounting for it: candidate 1 is measured on 18 in two arms. **Arm 17-equivalent** disables
  the skip-scan route by forcing the baseline to what 17 would do (`enable_indexscan` and
  `enable_bitmapscan` off, so the primary key cannot be probed); **arm 18 native** leaves the
  planner free. **The verdict is taken on the 17-equivalent arm** because that is production, and
  the 18 arm is reported so that a future upgrade is understood. If 18 native already matches the
  index, that is recorded as a note that the index's benefit is version-specific.
- Candidates 2 and 3 do not depend on skip scan.

## Method

1. Fresh cluster on port 55434, `alembic upgrade head` for the real schema, seed, `ANALYZE`.
2. Compile each production statement with the PostgreSQL dialect and literal binds. Bind values
   hit seeded rows (an account owning several organizations; a package that exists; the evidence
   horizon at the one-day boundary).
3. `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` each statement without the candidate index, then create the
   index, `ANALYZE`, and repeat. The `FOR UPDATE` and `DELETE` statements run inside
   `BEGIN ... ROLLBACK`.
4. Each arm: one cold-ish first run (reported separately), then **11 warm runs, median reported**.
   Planning time is excluded; execution time is the figure.
5. Insert overhead of the index is measured for candidate 3 (and noted for the others), because a
   background-only saving must also pay for itself on every evidence write.

## Materiality threshold (fixed now)

An index lands only if, **at hosted scale, on the PostgreSQL 17-equivalent arm**, all hold:

- (a) the plan for the production statement changes from a sequential (or full-index) scan of the
  target table to an Index or Bitmap scan that uses the candidate index with its predicate;
- (b) the warm median execution time falls by at least **50%**;
- (c) the absolute fall is at least **5 ms** for a statement on an interactive path or inside a
  lock-holding transaction (candidates 1 and 2), or at least **1 s per sweep pass** for the
  background retention delete (candidate 3). A large percentage on a sub-millisecond statement is
  not material;
- (d) for candidate 3 only, the measured write overhead per evidence insert does not exceed the
  per-pass saving, taken over a day of inserts.

For the `FOR UPDATE` statement, the benefit is read as the statement's execution time, which is the
time the transaction holds its row locks while the statement runs, not a smaller lock set.

Failing any limb means **no index lands** and the candidate is recorded as rejected with its
numbers. That is a complete outcome for D-01.

## If an index lands

Its own migration, chained linearly after `20260926_0034`, using `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` inside
`op.get_context().autocommit_block()` (PostgreSQL only; a no-op on SQLite as the sibling `rra_*`
migrations are). The model declares the index so `Base.metadata.create_all` agrees with the
migration. The migration head is pinned in three places and all three move together:
`tests/test_rca001_migration.py` `RCA_REVISIONS`, the `alembic heads` assertion in
`tests/test_rca001_session_persistence.py`, and `specs/001-rca-001-commercial-identity/STATUS.md`.
A test shows the index exists after upgrade and is gone after downgrade.

## Out of scope

No other schema change, no query rewrite, no other index, no change to the sweep cadence, and no
sizing decision: the assumed scales above are not proposals for `OPS1-09`.
